# Research report: adaptive resistance-training engine (Banister FFM, per-muscle fatigue, cheap readiness measures)

Tags: [RCT] randomised trial, [obs] observational/cohort, [model] modelling study, [animal], [expert] expert opinion/coach practice, [secondary] number taken from a review or blog, not the primary source, [weak] thin or contradictory evidence.

Human strength-athlete fits exist but report only correlations, no usable parameters: Busso 1990 (6 elite weightlifters, 1 yr, input training volume, output clean-and-jerk; fitness tracked testosterone r=0.73-0.99) doi:10.1007/BF00236693; Busso 1992 doi:10.1007/BF00636228; Busso 1994 hammer thrower (r=0.96, N=19 performances) doi:10.1007/BF00867927 [obs]. Abstracts retrieved via PubMed. Clarke & Skiba 2013, Adv Physiol Educ 37:134, doi:10.1152/advan.00078.2011 is the tutorial reference for the equations; full text was paywalled here, so the 1976 defaults above come from the secondary source.

Load input. No study compares volume load vs sets x RPE vs tonnage x intensity as FFM input for lifters. Related evidence: Mitchell 2020 (Eur J Sport Sci, doi:10.1080/17461391.2020.1719211) found 5-zone vs 7-zone load quantification made no difference to Banister/Busso prediction error in swimmers, and EWMA rolling loads fit as well as Banister (SEE 0.57-0.63 vs 0.62-0.64 s) [model]. Scott 2016 (Sports Med 46:687) reports sRPE-load tracks volume load more than load heaviness, so sRPE x minutes is mostly a volume proxy [secondary review]. Imbach 2025 (Sports 13:13, PMC11768794) proposes an intensity-weighted volume with exponential weighting of relative intensity, R2 0.68-0.77 vs fatigue markers in isolated exercise; verify the sign convention before implementing [model, weak for whole-body training].

Failure modes and fixes:
- Identifiability. tau1/tau2 correlate 0.99, k1/k2 0.91 (Hellard). Imbach 2025: with informative priors only 1-4 of 10 MCMC chains converge for the two-component model; the fitness-only model converges for every athlete and predicts held-out data equally well (RMSE difference 0.001, p = 0.57; 8.8e-5, p = 0.41). Their conclusion: fitness and fatigue parameters are antagonistic in estimation, and the fatigue term adds no predictive value from load alone. [model, strongest recent evidence]
- Data hunger. Nonlinear least squares on 14 performances gives meaningless CIs (Peng/Swartz). Liversedge (GoldenCheetah implementation blog, markliversedge.blogspot.com/2019/01) found tau1 and tau2 converge to identical or implausibly low values and made both user-tunable, fitting only k1, k2, p0; he reports needing about 20 maximal efforts, ideally over a season or more. [expert]
- Overfitting. Busso 2003 3-comp model never beat 2-comp; Philippe 2015 same. Fixed-k2 models cannot represent overreaching; the variable-k2 (Busso 2003) or Kalman state-noise (Kolossa) variants do.
- Kalman filter. Kolossa 2017 wrote the model as a linear time-variant state space (states F, G; observation p = p0 + F - G) and used measurement feedback to correct the states; error fell from 3.35% to 2.31% MAPE even with poorly identified taus. That is the practical fix for a lifter's sparse, noisy e1RM logs.

Recommended default for a single-lifter app: do not fit taus from logs. Fix tau_F = 40-45 d (Imbach prior 43.3, Busso 40.8, Hellard 38) and treat it as a prior, not a fact. For per-muscle G, tau = 7 d is too slow: with tau 7 d 65% of a session's fatigue impulse remains at 72 h, which contradicts the recovery data in section 3 (volume load back by 48 h, perception by 72 h). Use tau_Gmuscle 1.5-3 d (13-37% left at 72 h; Busso's tau3 for the fast fatigue factor was 2.3 d), and if a slow systemic G is kept (7-19 d), drive it from sRPE-load or Hooper score, not per-muscle sets. Estimate only p0 and the gains (k1 and the ratio theta = k2/k1 > 1, prior mean about 4 per Peng/Swartz) with a Kalman filter or Bayesian update on e1RM; hold theta fixed until roughly 20 performance points exist. Use a fitness-only model as the fallback and check on held-out sessions whether the fatigue term improves prediction, as Imbach did. Source the day-to-day fatigue state from section 2 measures rather than from load.

## 2. Cheap fatigue and readiness measures for lifters

| Measure | What it predicts | Noise / validity | Thresholds in use | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Session RPE load (Foster 1998, MSSE 30:1164, doi:10.1097/00005768-199807000-00023; Foster 2021, IJSPP 16:612, doi:10.1123/ijspp.2020-0599) | Internal load; illness when individual strain thresholds exceeded | CR-10 x minutes; rating stable from 1 min to 14 d after session; for RT it tracks volume load (McGuigan & Foster 2004 ICC 0.95 per Haddad 2017 review, PMC5673663) | Monotony = weekly mean/SD, strain = weekly load x monotony; monotony > 2.0 is the commonly quoted risk cutoff, individual strain thresholds only | [obs]; 2.0 cutoff [secondary] |
| RIR-based RPE (Zourdos 2016, JSCR 30:267, doi:10.1519/JSC.0000000000001049; Helms 2016, SCJ 38:42, PMC4961270) | Proximity to failure per set; velocity r = -0.88 experienced, -0.77 novice | Zourdos 2021 (JSCR, doi:10.1519/JSC.0000000000002995): in a 16-rep squat set at 70% 1RM, error 2.05 reps at 1 RIR, 3.65 at 3 RIR, about 5 at 5 RIR; accurate near failure, low reps, experienced lifters | Scale: RPE 10 = 0 RIR, 9.5 = could add load, 9 = 1, 8 = 2, 7 = 3, 5-6 = 4-6 RIR. Prescribe RPE 7-9; treat reported RIR > 3 as unreliable | [obs] |
| Hooper index (Hooper & Mackinnon 1995; 4 items sleep, stress, fatigue, soreness, 1-7, sum 4-28) | Wellness under load in team sports; correlates with training load | Campbell 2020 (Eur J Sport Sci 20:1395): total wellness did not distinguish low/mod/high intensity sessions; fatigue and soreness items did at 24 h (d 1.2-2.5) | No validated cutoff; GSSI monitoring review gives an illustrative rule of z <= -1.5 SD from the athlete's own baseline as "worth noting" | [obs]; cutoff [expert] |
| Perceived Recovery Status, 0-10 (Laurent 2011, JSCR) | Next-session performance: 0-2 expect decline, 3-7 no change, 8-10 improvement | Sikorski 2013 (JSCR 27:2079, doi:10.1519/JSC.0b013e31827e8e78): 8.6 -> 4.2 at 48 h after a whole-body high-volume session, r = 0.58 with CK. Tolusso 2022 (IJSPP, numbers via Sportsmith review [secondary]): after 8x10 squat at 70%, PRS 8.3 -> 4.6 (24 h), 4.8 (48 h), 6.2 (72 h); within-athlete r = 0.84 with CMJ, 0.80 with bar velocity | Use Laurent anchors: PRS <= 4 reduce or postpone the muscle group; >= 8 progress | [obs], best cheap measure |
| DOMS scale | Muscle damage, not performance | Impellizzeri & Maffiuletti 2007 (Clin J Sport Med 17:494, doi:10.1097/JSM.0b013e31815aed57): 7-point Likert vs 100 mm VAS r = 0.81, equal sensitivity over 96 h; VAS test-retest ICC 0.98 in a separate VAS-vs-pressure-pain study [secondary] | Log per muscle group on 0-10 or 1-7; soreness present at next session = RP "4" (section 3) | [obs] |
| Bar velocity loss | Within-set effort (lactate r 0.93-0.97, Sanchez-Medina 2011); between-day readiness via warm-up load-velocity | Mansfield 2023 (Eur J Sport Sci 23:2357, doi:10.1080/17461391.2023.2235314): 11-33% of reps faster than the previous one; individual velocity stops did not hit target RIR in recreational lifters | 20% VL stop preserves strength and power with 40% fewer reps (Pareja-Blanco 2017); >25% VL for hypertrophy (Jukic 2022 meta, PMC9807551) | [RCT] for VL stops; readiness use [weak], needs a device |
| Grip strength | Proposed CNS-fatigue proxy | Trail-race study: no change after a marathon-length effort (PMC12202473); Bell 2025 notes handgrip is a surrogate for dynamic strength, not validated for fatigue | none | [weak] |
| HRV (lnRMSSD) | Autonomic load | Flatt 2021 powerlifters (PMC7795557): morning lnRMSSD 82.9 -> 75.0 after overload, back to 81.9 after taper, but no association with bench 1RM/velocity/reps, limits of agreement +-15-17; PMC6835520: HRV back to baseline the next morning while CMJ needed 48 h and PRS/soreness were still down, no cross-correlations | none for individuals | [obs], not useful for a lifter |

Recommended default: log PRS (0-10) and per-muscle soreness (0-10) before each session, set RPE (RIR-based) after each work set, and a Hooper-style sleep/stress pair daily. Derive readiness as the athlete's own z-score of PRS over a 4-week rolling window; flag z <= -1.5 or PRS <= 4. Skip HRV and grip. Treat velocity as optional.

## 3. Per-muscle recovery and Renaissance Periodization landmarks

Recovery time courses (all lower-body unless noted):
- Goulart 2020 (Eur J Sport Sci 21:935, doi:10.1080/17461391.2020.1789227): 14 trained men, 5 x 8-10RM squat + leg press to failure. Volume load reduced at 24 h (ES 0.90), not at 48 h (ES 0.22) or 72 h; first-set volume load still down at 48 h (ES 0.63); DOMS up at 48 h (ES 3.5); CMJ and MVIC slightly above baseline at 72 h. Conclusion: 48 h minimum, 72 h better perceptually. [obs]
- Bartolomei 2017 (Eur J Appl Physiol 117:1287, doi:10.1007/s00421-017-3598-9): 12 trained men, squat 8x10 at 70% vs 8x3 at 90%. High volume: MVIC -23.6% at 30 min, still -11% at 72 h; CMJ power -15.9% / -9.6% / -7.8% at 30 min / 24 h / 48 h; soreness higher than high intensity at every point to 72 h. High intensity: CMJ -5.5% at 30 min only, MVIC back by 24 h. Volume, not intensity, sets recovery time. [obs]
- Judge & Burke 2010 (IJSPP 5:184, doi:10.1123/ijspp.5.2.184): bench press 5 sets to a 5RM; men's e1RM down at 4 and 24 h, recovered by 48 h; women showed no decrement. [obs, small n]
- Bartolomei 2021 (JSCR 35:S180, doi:10.1519/JSC.0000000000002960): bench 8x10 at 70%, passive recovery: bench throw power and isometric bench down at 15 min and 24 h, recovered by 48 h; pec thickness still altered at 48 h. Light bench sessions at 6 h and 30 h restored performance by 15 min post. [obs]
- Bell 2025 deloading review (SCJ, doras.dcu.ie/31501): coach practice for lift-specific rest before competition: deadlift 7-10 d, squat 4-7 d, bench 2-4 d. [expert]
Working numbers: upper-body push recovers in 24-48 h, high-volume lower body 48-72 h with residual strength loss to 72 h, heavy low-volume lower body 24 h. No study gives per-muscle taus; the app must learn them from soreness and first-set performance.

The landmarks hub (rpstrength.com/blogs/articles/training-volume-landmarks-muscle-growth) gives a generic MV of about 6 sets/wk at 2x frequency and the example mesocycle 12 -> 14 -> 16 -> 18 -> 20 sets over 5 weeks, then a deload at 6 sets.

RP set-progression algorithm as published on that hub page (verbatim scales): soreness 1 = no soreness, 2 = healed well before next session, 3 = healed just in time, 4 = still sore at next session; performance 1 = exceeded targets easily, 2 = hit targets, 3 = struggled, 4 = could not match last week. Rules: both 1 -> add 2-3 sets; 2s or mix of 1-2 -> add 1 set; any 3, or soreness 3-4 -> hold volume; performance 4 -> recovery session or deload. Pump and joint pain are app inputs but no public rule maps them. [expert] Independent evidence for MEV/MRV numbers is indirect: 6-9 sets/wk gave the same squat 1RM gains as 18-45 sets (examine.com summary of Androulakis-Korakakis 2021 and related) [secondary], and Coleman 2024 found no hypertrophy difference at about 90 sets/wk with or without a deload.

Recommended default: seed per-muscle weekly set targets at RP MEV, cap at RP MRV, add 1 set/wk when the RP score is 1-2 on both axes, hold on any 3, and treat performance 4 or PRS <= 4 as the per-muscle deload trigger. Initialise per-muscle fatigue tau at 2 d for upper-body pushing and arms, 2.5 d for quads/glutes/hamstrings/back, and adjust from the lifter's own soreness-clearance day (RP soreness 3 means fatigue near zero at the next session; 4 means it was not).

## 4. Autoregulation rules with evidence

- RPE-based load: Helms 2018 (Front Physiol 9:247, doi:10.3389/fphys.2018.00247), 21 trained men, 8 wk. Rule: for every 0.5 RPE outside the target range, change load 2% on the next set. RPE group +17.1 vs +13.9 kg squat (ES 0.50), +10.7 vs +9.6 kg bench (ES 0.28). Same rule recommended by MASS. [RCT, small]
- Autoregulated vs fixed progression: Mann 2010 (JSCR 24:1718, doi:10.1519/JSC.0b013e3181def4a6) APRE beat linear periodization in bench 1RM (+93 vs 0 N) and squat e1RM (+193 vs +37 N) over 6 wk [RCT]; Graham & Cleather 2019 (JSCR) RIR-based loading beat fixed loading over 12 wk [RCT]; a 2021 meta-analysis (PMC7994759) finds a small advantage for autoregulation overall. APRE's own rule: adjust next set by +/-2.5-5 kg (upper) or 5-10 kg (lower) based on reps achieved in a max-rep set [expert protocol].
- Velocity stops: 20% VL matched 40% VL for squat 1RM and beat it for CMJ (9.5 vs 3.5%) with 40% fewer reps, Pareja-Blanco 2017 (Scand J Med Sci Sports 27:724, doi:10.1111/sms.12678) [RCT]. Jukic 2022 meta: VL threshold does not change strength gains; higher VL (>25%) favours hypertrophy; <= 15-25% preserves jump/sprint. Rodriguez-Rosell 2021: 10/30/45% VL groups, 3 sets each. [RCT]
- Progression increments: ACSM 2009 position stand (MSSE 41:687, doi:10.1249/MSS.0b013e3181915670): raise load 2-10% when 1-2 reps over target on two consecutive sessions, smaller for small muscle groups [guideline]. NSCA "2-for-2 rule": 2.5-5% (2.5-5 lb) upper, 5-10% (5-10 lb) lower [guideline, secondary]. Double progression (fill a rep range, then add load) is universal practice with no trial testing it against alternatives [expert].
- Deloads: Bell 2024 survey (Sports Med Open, doi:10.1186/s40798-024-00691-y), 246 strength/physique athletes: deload every 5.6 +- 2.3 wk for 6.4 +- 1.7 d; 78.9% cut sets, 83.7% cut load, 84.9% raised RIR; triggers were plateaus, soreness, joint pain; 47% pre-planned, 39% mixed, 13% purely reactive [obs]. Bell 2023 Delphi consensus (Sports Med Open 9:87, doi:10.1186/s40798-023-00633-0): cut volume 30-50%, cut load about 10% or add 1-3 RIR, keep frequency [expert Delphi]. Bell et al. practical-approach paper (SCJ, doras.dcu.ie/31501): volume -25-45% (low recovery need), -40-60% (moderate), -60-90% (high); 5-7 d; every 4-8 wk; reactive deloads may be one session [expert]. Coleman 2024 (PeerJ 12:e16777, doi:10.7717/peerj.16777): a full week off at week 5 of 9 gave the same hypertrophy and a non-significant trend to less strength (squat CI -3.0 to 12.1 kg favouring continuous, p 0.149) [RCT, weak]. No study validates a numeric performance-drop trigger.

Recommended default: prescribe by RIR-based RPE range (e.g. 7-9); adjust load 2% per 0.5 RPE miss within a session, cap at +-6%. Between sessions use double progression with ACSM increments (2.5% or the smallest plate for upper body, 5% for lower body) when the top rep target is hit at or below target RPE twice running. Reactive per-muscle deload (one session at 50% sets, +2 RIR) when RP performance = 4 or PRS <= 4; whole-body deload (5-7 d, sets -40%, load -10%) when e1RM on two main lifts is below the model's 4-week median for two consecutive sessions or when a pre-planned 5-6 week block ends. Velocity stops only if a device exists.
